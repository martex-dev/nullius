The claim under test

{{ paper.claim }}

Abstract

An artificial research institution was built with preregistration, an evaluation custodian, adversarial detectors, independent replication and evidence-typed memory, each switchable, and measured against a question bank whose ground truth was planted and locked before any of it ran. Across {{ paper.chapters | length }} registered protocols, {{ paper.predictions_refuted }} predictions were refuted and {{ paper.predictions_upheld }} upheld.

The headline result reversed twice under measurement, both times because running a protocol exposed a flaw in it, and two published results are retracted by later protocols inside this same document. What survived every revision is not the accuracy ordering but the calibration ordering, and one mechanism: spending experimental seeds where they change an answer.

The question bank

Ground truth is planted rather than judged. Each item is a data generating process whose true effect the oracle measures at forty seeds of twenty thousand samples, resolving it to about 0.0008; an experiment gets five seeds of two thousand and resolves it to about {{ measured_se }}. That gap is the room the bank has: an item can sit inside one experiment standard error of a verdict boundary while remaining several oracle standard errors clear of it, so the question is genuinely hard and its answer is not in doubt.

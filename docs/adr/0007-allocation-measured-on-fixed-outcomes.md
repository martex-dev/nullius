# ADR-0007 — Allocation is measured over pre-measured outcomes, not by re-running the science

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-31
- **Relates to:** `BUILD_PLAN.md` M9, `docs/02-architecture.md` §7, `docs/04-evaluation.md`

## Context

M9's acceptance criterion is that greedy-EIG *measurably beats random on cost-per-correct-claim over the bank, or is shown not to*. The obvious way to run that comparison is to give each policy its own budget and let it conduct its own research programme over the bank.

That design is wrong for two reasons, one practical and one fatal.

**Practical.** A full pass through the lifecycle costs about ten seconds per bank item — twenty items, five seeds each, three arms, all executed in the sandbox. Four policies times a bootstrap over items is thousands of programmes. It would not run in CI, so it would not run.

**Fatal.** If each policy runs its own experiments, the arms differ in *what was executed* as well as in *what was chosen*. A gap in cost-per-correct-claim could then come from the allocator or from seed variance in the experiments the allocator happened to commission, and nothing in the design separates them. The project's own design linter refuses experiments with this shape, and the benchmark should not be allowed to ship one.

A third problem sits underneath both. Without an API key the roles run against `MockProvider`, which is free. Cost-per-correct-claim with a numerator of exactly zero is not a metric.

## Decision

**Measure once, allocate many times.** Every bank item is carried through the lifecycle exactly once, and its verdict, its cost, and the forecasts locked before it ran are written to `bank/outcomes.lock.json` — committed, hashed against the bank items, and regenerated by `nullius economy measure`. The policies then choose among those measured outcomes under a budget. Every arm sees identical items with identical costs and identical results, so the only thing that varies is selection.

**Price the mock's tokens as if they were a real model's.** Token *counts* are real measurements of what the roles sent and received; the rate applied to them is `AS_IF_MODEL`, named in the lock file so a reader can reprice. Compute cost is not substituted at all: those seconds were genuinely spent, and `ExperimentRunner` now charges the cost ledger for them.

**Ship a cost-only control policy.** `CheapestFirst` ranks on expected cost and nothing else. Any policy that divides by cost will look good on a cost-denominated metric, so without this control "greedy-EIG beat random" cannot be distinguished from "having a denominator beat random". This is the same argument the design linter makes when it requires a capacity-matched arm.

**Report two metrics, and the spread of the information term.** Cost-per-correct-claim is the milestone's metric; nats-per-dollar is what greedy-EIG actually maximises. They are different objectives and may point in opposite directions. Alongside them, the standard deviation of EIG across items is reported first, because when it is zero no policy could have ranked on information and the rest of the table is about something else.

## Consequences

**Good.** The comparison isolates allocation as a cause, runs in seconds, and is reproducible from a committed file. The ground truth and the measured outcomes are both locked and both regenerable, so neither can drift silently.

**Bad, and stated rather than hidden.** This measures *selection only*. A real allocator also affects how much is spent on each experiment — more seeds, another replication — and that channel is invisible here. A policy that improves research by spending differently *within* an item would score as no better than random.

**Also bad.** The recorded forecasts under a mock provider are identical for every item, so the information term is constant and greedy-EIG degenerates to the cost-only control. Reporting "EIG does not help" from that setting would be a claim about the forecasts dressed up as a claim about the policy. `nullius economy sweep` therefore replaces the recorded forecasts with synthetic ones whose per-item information content is dialled from nothing to oracle-grade, and reports the forecast quality at which the gap opens — if it opens. The top of that ladder uses ground truth no role may see and is an upper bound, never a result.

**The trigger to revisit.** The first live run with a real API key. At that point the recorded forecasts carry genuine per-item information, the sweep's λ can be estimated rather than assumed, and the headline comparison can be re-run on forecasts that were actually elicited.
